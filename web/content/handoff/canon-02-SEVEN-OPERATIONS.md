# The seven operations

The seven operations
 1. Input — What actually occurred, or what material is present, before I explain what it means?

 2. Decomposition — What parts of this are event, behavior, interpretation, emotion, judgment, assumption,
 context, identity attribution, and unknown?

 3. Contextual comparison — Where has something structurally similar appeared before, and what was the same or
 different about the context?

 4. Pattern detection — What appears to repeat across the available material, and under what conditions does that
 repetition appear?

 5. Contradiction detection — What parts of the available material appear inconsistent, incompatible, revised, or
 unresolved when compared with one another?

 6. Evidentiary separation — What is directly evidenced here, what is interpreted or inferred, what remains
 hypothetical or symbolic, and what is presently unknown?

 7. Observational report — After separating all of this material, what can actually be observed without deciding what
 I should think, become, choose, or do?

 Then stop. Human choice begins outside the engine.
